Why it's fair

Built so nobody gets played

01

Your wallet, your SOL

No sign-up, no broker. Every bet sits in an on-chain account and pays straight back to your wallet.

02

Nobody is owed what isn't there

Winners are paid from the losing side's pool, never from thin air. No bad debt, no liquidations. You can't lose more than you put in.

03

Always someone on the other side

The PULSE House seeds both sides of every question and the Backstop takes the thin side of perps, so you can play even when nobody else is online.

04

Numbers anyone can check

Readings come from public sources (CoinGecko, Steam, Open-Meteo and more), move at most 15% per update, and a public auditor checks them every hour.

FAQ

Questions people ask

What can I bet on?

Crypto prices (BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, XRP, DOGE), total crypto market cap and Bitcoin dominance, Crypto Fear & Greed, Solana TPS, money flowing into crypto narratives, live player counts of big games, city temperatures, Wikipedia views and GitHub momentum. New questions open every hour.

How do predictions work?

Every question is YES or NO about a live number, like "Will Bitcoin be above $95,000 on Sunday?". Your SOL goes into the YES pool or the NO pool, and the % you see is YES's share of all the money. When the question ends, the program reads the first audited number after the deadline and the winning side splits the whole pot by stake (minus 1%). Winnings land in your wallet automatically. If nobody took the other side, everyone is refunded. The PULSE House opens new questions all the time and puts SOL on both sides, so there are always odds and always someone to bet against.

Can I make my own question?

Yes. Tap Ask, pick any market, choose above or below a number and when it ends, then place the first bet. Share the link and anyone can take the other side.

Where does the data come from? Can anyone fake it?

A keeper reads Steam's public player-count API every minute and posts it on-chain. Cheating is pointless by design: the program caps how far any reading can move (15%), and you trade the 24-hour average, so one fake reading moves the price by about 0.01%. Faking a real move would take hours in public, and you can exit any time. A public auditor also checks every on-chain reading against Steam every hour (see the audit log). The program is built to plug in a fully verified feed (zkTLS) for mainnet.

Why a 24-hour average?

Every game peaks and dips at the same hours each day. If you traded the raw number, people could farm that predictable cycle. The program averages the last 24 hours on-chain, so the daily cycle cancels out and only real trends move the price: a game growing, dying, or blowing up at launch.

Who do I win from if nobody else is trading?

The PULSE Backstop. It automatically takes the opposite side of one-sided markets (up to 0.5 SOL per market on devnet). On mainnet it becomes a vault anyone can deposit into to earn trading fees.

How do duels work?

Pick a market, call ABOVE or BELOW a number at a time you choose, and lock your SOL. Anyone can take the other side by matching your stake. After the deadline the program reads the first audited reading and pays the winner the whole pot (minus a 1% fee). Nobody accepts? You get your SOL back. Share the duel link to challenge a friend, or make it private: only people with your join code can take it. Every duel and market has its own on-chain chat.

Can I get liquidated?

No. You hold a share of a pool. It shrinks when you're wrong, but it never goes below zero and nobody force-closes you.

What do I trade with?

Not yet. PULSE runs on Solana devnet, so you trade with devnet SOL, which is free. Tap "Get free devnet SOL" inside any market.

Why does my order take a couple of minutes?

Fair fills. Your order fills at the price after the next update, so nobody can trade on data they already saw.
